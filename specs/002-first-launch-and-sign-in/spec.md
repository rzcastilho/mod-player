# Feature Specification: First Launch Disclosure and Sign-In

**Feature Branch**: `feature/002-first-launch-and-sign-in`

**Created**: 2026-09-15

**Status**: Clarified (2026-09-15) — no open human decisions

**Input**: User description: "Let a new user go from a fresh install to a signed-in account, with the legal position stated plainly before anything else happens. ModPlayer connects to the user's Spotify Premium account through an unofficial receiver protocol; the user must understand that before they sign in. On first launch the app shows a welcome screen with a one-paragraph description of the product, a plain-language disclosure that the client connects to the streaming service in an unofficial way, that a Premium subscription is required, and that the user's account is subject to the service's terms, plus an explicit "I understand, continue" action. Declining closes the app with a short explanation. The acknowledgement is recorded locally together with the version of the disclosure text, and the disclosure is shown again whenever that text changes in a later release. A plain-language privacy notice is reachable from the welcome screen and from About. The product name, icon, and branding never use the streaming service's trademarks. Sign-in uses the service's own browser-based authorization flow opened in the system default browser; the app never presents a password field. The resulting session credential is stored only in the operating system's secure credential store. After authorization the app shows a "checking your account" state, then verifies the tier: a Premium account proceeds to the audio device check; a non-Premium account sees an explanation with a link to the service's upgrade page and can stay signed in to browse but not play. Only one account is signed in at a time. While online the credential is refreshed before expiry; if refresh keeps failing, a non-blocking notice offers "sign in again" and playback continues on the existing credential until it expires. Sign-out clears the session credential, the offline cache, and all account-scoped state within seconds, after a confirmation that lists what is deleted; plugin installs and non-account settings remain. Acceptance: when the user cancels the browser authorization or it times out, the app returns to the sign-in step with a retry action and no partial state. When the OS secure store is locked or unavailable, sign-in is refused with platform-specific guidance and never falls back to plain-file storage. When authorization completes in the browser after the app was closed, the next launch picks up the result if still valid, otherwise restarts sign-in. When the service revokes the session remotely, the app shows a critical notice, clears the credential, and offers sign-in."

**Source**: [ModPlayer-Software-Specification.md](../autonomous/ModPlayer-Software-Specification.md) — J-1, FR-1.1, FR-1.2, INT-1, DM-1, DM-27, EC §1, NFR §4/§5/§11, GOV §6. Prerequisite: 001-walking-skeleton (app shell, settings screen).

**Scope boundary**: Does not cover the Connect receiver, playback engine, library browsing, or the offline grace period — only disclosure, authorization, tier check, credential storage, and sign-out.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-15

Resolution ladder applied: (D) derived from an authoritative source, (A) assumed conventional default. Every item below is also encoded in the requirement text; this section records the decision and its source or rationale. No item met the materiality bar for human escalation: the source document's open questions touching this slice (Q-5, Q-16) are defaulted reversibly below, and A-2 is a planning-phase spike (see Assumptions), not a product decision.

- Q: Where does the disclosure sit relative to 001's first-launch Device Check? → A (D, J-1 steps 1–4; 001 FR-002): Launch order is **Welcome/disclosure** (shown when no acknowledgement for the current disclosure version is recorded) → **Sign-in step** (shown when no AccountSession exists) → **Device Check** (001's rule "no confirmed device", now evaluated only when the session tier is Premium) → **main window**. A non-Premium or unknown-tier session skips Device Check (there is nothing to play); 001's rule applies unchanged once the tier becomes Premium. Device Check on demand from Settings › Audio is unaffected.
- Q: Is sign-in mandatory to reach the main window? → A (D, J-1 step 2, FR-1.2, FR-1.2.5): Yes. There is no skip, guest, or "later" path. Every signed-out state (fresh, after sign-out, after revocation) shows the sign-in step full-window instead of the main window. Automated tests exercise the flow through test doubles of the authorization service and of the OS secure store, so no live service or unlocked keychain is needed in CI (Constitution IV/VIII by analogy).
- Q: What is the disclosure "version" and what changes it? → A (D, FR-1.1.3, NFR-11.4; A for mechanism): a single explicit **disclosure bundle version** identifier shipped with the text, covering both the disclosure and the privacy notice. It is bumped only when the substantive content of either changes in any locale's source; typo fixes and translations of unchanged content do not bump it. The welcome screen is re-shown when the recorded version is **not equal** to the current one. A snapshot test pins the en-US disclosure and privacy-notice source text to the version, so CI fails if the text changes without a bump. Rationale: an explicit version lets legal wording be corrected without re-prompting every user; the snapshot guard makes forgetting the bump impossible.
- Q: Q-5 — waiting period or link to the service's terms? → A (A): **no waiting period**; the acknowledgement action is enabled immediately. The welcome screen includes a link to the streaming service's terms (opens in the system browser) and a link to the in-app privacy notice. Rationale: a waiting period is trivially added later by shipping new text under a new version (which re-prompts everyone); the terms link is the conventional, zero-cost part of Q-5. Legal review (Q-4) may revise the text; all of it is externalized.
- Q: Q-16 — may the UI name the streaming service? → A (D, NFR-11.3 "references are descriptive"; breakdown prompt names the service): body text may name the service **descriptively** (e.g. "connects to Spotify through an unofficial protocol"); the product name, icon, wordmark, application identifier, and branding never contain the service's name or marks. Strings are externalized so a different legal outcome changes only text.
- Q: What does "declining closes the app" look like? → A (D, J-1 DP-1.1; A for control): declining replaces the welcome screen with a short explanation that the client cannot function without the acknowledgement and a single **Quit** action; the app exits when it is activated. Nothing is written (no acknowledgement, no session, no settings change); the next launch shows the welcome screen again. Rationale: an explicit Quit keeps the explanation readable by assistive technology; auto-closing would not.
- Q: Where is the acknowledgement stored, and is it account-scoped? → A (D, DM-27 is a separate entity from DM-1; retention table lists settings as "not cleared by sign-out"; 001 FR-020): DisclosureAcknowledgement (version + acknowledged-at timestamp) is stored in the per-user settings store (atomic replace, as in 001) and is **device-scoped, not account-scoped**: it survives sign-out and revocation. DM-1's "disclosure acknowledged version" attribute is a read-only mirror of DM-27, not a second record. Rationale: the acknowledgement is a per-installation legal fact; scoping it to the account would re-prompt on every sign-out.
- Q: How does the app wait for the browser, and for how long? → A (D, INT-1.1, ERR-1.3; A for values): after opening the system browser the app shows a "Waiting for your browser…" state with **Cancel** and **Open the browser again** actions. The attempt times out **5 minutes** after the browser was opened. Cancel (in-app or reported by the service), timeout, or a definitive error from the service all return to the sign-in step with a **Retry** action and a one-line note of what happened. At most one authorization attempt is pending at a time; starting a new one discards the previous. Rationale: 5 minutes comfortably exceeds a normal browser sign-in and is well inside typical authorization-code validity.
- Q: What is "pending result" / "no partial state" concretely? → A (D, EC-1.1, Constitution VI; A for the age bound): the transient state needed to validate and complete an authorization result (the attempt's identifier/secret, started-at time) is held **only in the OS secure store** and deleted on completion, cancel, timeout, or when a new attempt starts. No AccountSession and no credential exists until the service has exchanged the result successfully. On launch, if pending state exists and is younger than 5 minutes, the app attempts to complete it; on success the user is signed in and the tier check runs; otherwise the pending state is deleted and the sign-in step is shown with the note "Your previous sign-in didn't finish".
- Q: When and how is the secure store checked? → A (D, EC-1.6, Constitution VI; A for mechanism): **before opening the browser**, the app performs a write-read-delete round-trip of a probe entry in the OS secure store. On failure, sign-in is refused; the message names the platform's store (macOS: Keychain; Windows: Credential Manager; Linux: the Secret Service provider, e.g. GNOME Keyring or KWallet), gives one remediation step (unlock/enable it), and offers **Retry**. At launch, if a session exists but the store cannot be read, the app shows the sign-in step with a **warning** notice "Couldn't read your saved sign-in — unlock your <store> and retry" and a Retry action; nothing is cleared and this is **not** treated as revocation.
- Q: What if the tier check itself fails? → A (D, DM-1 tier enum includes `unknown`; A for values): "Checking your account" times out after **30 seconds**. On timeout or a transport/network error the credential is **kept**, tier is set to `unknown`, and the screen shows "Couldn't verify your subscription" with **Retry**. Playback is permitted iff tier is `premium`; `unknown` is gated like non-Premium but the message says "couldn't verify", never "not Premium". The tier can be re-checked from Settings › Account at any time.
- Q: What does non-Premium "browse but not play" mean in this slice? → A (D, ERR-1.2, FR-1.2.3; A for scope): the non-Premium screen shows the explanation, **Open upgrade page** (system browser), and **Continue**, which opens the main window. The session exposes a single fact, *playback permitted* = (tier == `premium`), that later slices gate streaming playback on. This slice does **not** gate 001's synthetic test track (it is not service content). Settings › Account shows the tier and a **Re-check subscription** action.
- Q: Refresh timing, backoff, and "fails repeatedly"? → A (D, INT-1.2, FR-1.2.6; A for values): while online, refresh is attempted no later than **5 minutes before expiry** (or at half the remaining lifetime if the credential's total lifetime is under 10 minutes). On a transport/network failure the app retries with exponential backoff starting at 1 s and capped at 60 s. After **3 consecutive failures** a **warning** notification "Sign in again" with that action is raised (persists until dismissed, per 001); retries continue in the background and the notification is cleared automatically when a refresh succeeds. The existing credential is kept and used until it actually expires. A definitive rejection (the service says the credential is invalid or revoked, as opposed to a transport error) is the revocation path, not backoff.
- Q: What counts as remote revocation, and when is it detected? → A (D, EC-1.3, INT-1.3, INT-1 "validation on launch"): detected on launch validation (when online), on refresh, or on any service call that returns a definitive credential-invalid/revoked response. Action: remove the credential from the secure store, delete AccountSession and all account-scoped state (including the offline cache once it exists), and show the sign-in step with a **critical** notice "Your session was revoked by the streaming service" and a **Sign in** action. Stopping playback at the end of the current track is the playback slice's responsibility.
- Q: What happens when the credential expires and cannot be refreshed? → A (D, DM-1 lifecycle `active → expired`, EC-1.2): AccountSession enters `expired`; the credential is **retained** (the offline grace period in a later slice depends on it); the main window stays available; a **critical** notification "Session expired — sign in again" with that action is raised. Sign in again opens the sign-in step and, on success, replaces the credential. The disclosure is not re-shown. Expiry clears nothing; revocation clears everything — the two are distinct.
- Q: Where do sign-in, sign-out, and account status live after first launch? → A (D, 001 Settings › Account placeholder, FR-1.2.4, NFR-5.4): Settings › Account shows the display name, tier, last online validation time, **Re-check subscription**, and **Sign out**; when signed out it shows **Sign in**. Sign-out opens a confirmation listing the categories that will be deleted; on confirm the credential is removed from the secure store, AccountSession and all account-scoped state are deleted, the sign-in step is shown, and an **info** notification reports "Signed out. Deleted: <categories>". In this slice the categories are: *your sign-in credential* and *account details (identifier, display name, subscription tier, expiry)*. The list is generated from the registry of account-scoped stores present in the build, so the offline cache and per-track state appear automatically when their slices land. Plugin installs, non-account settings, and the disclosure acknowledgement are untouched.
- Q: Where is non-secret session data stored? → A (A): account identifier, display name, tier, credential expiry, and last online validation time are stored in the per-user app state (plain, atomic replace), tagged account-scoped and deleted on sign-out/revocation. Only a reference to the credential is stored there — never the credential value.
- Q: Privacy notice and About presentation? → A (D, NFR-5.5, 001 "en-US only"): the privacy notice is an in-app text screen (readable offline), externalized like every other string; en-US is the only shipped locale in this slice. About shows the product description, version, **Privacy notice**, and **Disclosure** (re-read only; no re-acknowledgement). The welcome screen's privacy link opens the same screen.
- Q: Accessibility, keyboard operability, string externalization? → A (D, Constitution X, NFR-6.1/6.2, NFR-7.1; same as 001): every control introduced here (welcome, decline, sign-in, waiting, tier result, Settings › Account, sign-out confirmation, notices) is keyboard-operable and exposes an accessible name, role, and state; all strings are externalized.
- Q: How is "credential never in a plain file or log" (SC-004) verified? → A (D, Constitution VI/VIII): automated tests run the whole flow with a secure-store double and a fake authorization service, then assert the credential value appears in no settings/state file, log line, or notification text; a second test asserts sign-in is refused when the store double reports unavailable and that no file is written.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - First-launch disclosure (Priority: P1)

A person installs ModPlayer for the first time and opens it. Before anything else happens, they see a welcome screen that plainly states what the product is, that it connects to their streaming account in an unofficial way, that a paid subscription tier is required, and that their account remains subject to the streaming service's own terms. They must actively acknowledge this before the app does anything else; declining closes the app.

**Why this priority**: This is the legal and trust foundation of the whole app. No other feature — sign-in, playback, plugins — may execute before this disclosure is shown and acknowledged. Without it, the product cannot ship responsibly.

**Independent Test**: Fresh install, launch the app with no prior local state, and confirm the welcome screen with disclosure text and an acknowledgement action appears before any account or playback UI, and that declining exits the app with an explanation and leaves no signed-in state.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a fresh install with no local disclosure record, **When** the user launches the app, **Then** the app shows a welcome screen — before the Device Check of 001 and before any account UI — with a one-paragraph product description, an unofficial-connection disclosure, a Premium-required statement, a "subject to the streaming service's terms" statement, a link to the service's terms, a link to the privacy notice, an explicit "I understand, continue" action (enabled immediately, no waiting period), and a decline action; no other interactive path is available.
2. **Given** the welcome screen is shown, **When** the user chooses the acknowledgement action, **Then** the app records the acknowledgement locally (current disclosure bundle version + timestamp, device-scoped) and proceeds to the sign-in step.
3. **Given** the welcome screen is shown, **When** the user declines instead of acknowledging, **Then** the app shows a short explanation with a single **Quit** action, exits when it is activated, writes nothing (no acknowledgement, no session), and shows the welcome screen again on the next launch.
4. **Given** the user already acknowledged a prior disclosure bundle version, **When** a later release ships a changed disclosure or privacy-notice text under a new version, **Then** the app shows the welcome screen again on next launch instead of skipping straight to sign-in; a release that changes only typos or translations keeps the version and does not re-prompt.
5. **Given** the user already acknowledged the current disclosure bundle version, **When** they relaunch the app, **Then** the app does not show the welcome screen again and proceeds directly to the sign-in step (no session) or the signed-in flow.
6. **Given** the user acknowledged the disclosure and later signs out, **When** they relaunch or start sign-in again, **Then** the welcome screen is not shown again (the acknowledgement survives sign-out).
7. **Given** the welcome screen or About is shown, **When** the user opens the privacy notice, **Then** the same in-app privacy notice text is shown, readable without network access.

### User Story 2 - Sign-in and tier verification (Priority: P1)

After acknowledging the disclosure, the user signs in to their streaming account using that service's own browser-based login, without ever typing a password into ModPlayer. Once authorized, the app checks whether the account has the required subscription tier and either proceeds (Premium) or explains the limitation (non-Premium, browse-only).

**Why this priority**: Sign-in is the gate to every other feature in the product; without it the user cannot reach playback, library, or plugins. It must be correct, secure, and resilient to interruption on day one.

**Independent Test**: From the post-disclosure state, trigger sign-in, complete authorization in a browser, and confirm the app reaches either the audio-device-check step (Premium account) or a browse-only explanation state (non-Premium account), with the credential stored only in the OS secure store.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the user has acknowledged the disclosure and is not signed in, **When** they start sign-in, **Then** the app first verifies the OS secure store with a write-read-delete probe, then opens the streaming service's browser-based authorization flow in the system default browser, shows a "Waiting for your browser…" state with **Cancel** and **Open the browser again**, and presents no password field anywhere in the app.
2. **Given** the browser authorization completes successfully, **When** the app regains control, **Then** it stores the resulting session credential only in the operating system's secure credential store (never a plain file), deletes the transient authorization state, and shows a "Checking your account" state before any further sign-in-dependent UI.
3. **Given** the account tier check completes and the account is Premium, **When** the check finishes, **Then** the app proceeds to the Device Check step (001's "no confirmed device" rule) and then the main window.
4. **Given** the account tier check completes and the account is not Premium, **When** the check finishes, **Then** the app shows an explanation with **Open upgrade page** (system browser) and **Continue**, keeps the user signed in, opens the main window on Continue, skips Device Check, and exposes *playback permitted = false* for later slices to gate on.
5. **Given** the tier check times out (30 s) or fails with a network error, **When** the failure is detected, **Then** the credential is kept, the tier is `unknown`, the screen says "Couldn't verify your subscription" with **Retry**, and playback is not permitted until a re-check reports Premium.
6. **Given** an account is already signed in, **When** the user looks for a way to sign in as another account, **Then** the only path offered is Settings › Account › **Sign out** followed by sign-in; no add-account or switch-account action exists.
7. **Given** the user is signed in and online, **When** the stored credential is within 5 minutes of expiry (or half its remaining lifetime if the total lifetime is under 10 minutes), **Then** the app refreshes it automatically before it expires.
8. **Given** automatic credential refresh has failed 3 consecutive times with transport errors, **When** the app is still online, **Then** it raises a non-blocking warning notification "Sign in again" with that action, keeps retrying with backoff (1 s doubling to a 60 s cap), continues using the existing credential until it actually expires, and clears the notification automatically when a refresh succeeds.
9. **Given** the user is signed in, **When** they open Settings › Account, **Then** they see the display name, subscription tier, last online validation time, **Re-check subscription**, and **Sign out**.

### User Story 3 - Sign-out (Priority: P2)

A signed-in user wants to remove their account from the app — for example, before handing off the device or switching accounts. Sign-out is explicit, confirmed, and thorough: it removes the account's credential, cached data, and account-scoped settings, but leaves installed plugins and non-account settings untouched.

**Why this priority**: Sign-out is less frequently used than sign-in but is a correctness- and privacy-critical operation: leaving stale credentials or cached account data behind after a user asks to leave the account would violate the product's security and privacy posture.

**Independent Test**: From a signed-in state with some cached account data, trigger sign-out, confirm the listed deletions, and verify within seconds that the credential, offline cache, and account-scoped state are gone while plugin installs and non-account settings remain.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a signed-in user, **When** they choose Settings › Account › **Sign out**, **Then** the app shows a confirmation listing every account-scoped category that will be deleted (in this slice: *your sign-in credential* and *account details*; the offline cache and per-track state join the list when their slices exist) with **Sign out** and **Cancel**.
2. **Given** the user confirms sign-out, **When** the operation completes, **Then** within 10 seconds the session credential is removed from the OS secure store, AccountSession and all account-scoped state are deleted, any in-flight refresh is abandoned, the sign-in step is shown, and an info notification reports "Signed out. Deleted: <categories>".
3. **Given** sign-out has completed, **When** the user inspects installed plugins, non-account settings, and the disclosure acknowledgement, **Then** those remain unchanged and the welcome screen is not re-shown.

### User Story 4 - Interrupted or revoked authorization recovery (Priority: P2)

Authorization does not always complete cleanly: the user may cancel the browser flow, it may time out, the app may be closed mid-flow, or the streaming service may revoke the session remotely after the fact. The app must recover from each of these without leaving partial or confusing state.

**Why this priority**: These are the edge cases explicitly called out as acceptance criteria in the source request; handling them correctly is what separates a robust first-run flow from one that strands users in a broken state.

**Independent Test**: Individually simulate cancellation, timeout, app-closed-during-auth, and remote revocation, and confirm each resolves to a clean, well-explained state with no orphaned session or cache data.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the user is in the browser authorization flow, **When** they press the in-app **Cancel**, cancel in the browser, or 5 minutes pass without a result, **Then** the app returns to the sign-in step with a **Retry** action and a one-line note of what happened, the transient authorization state is deleted, and no AccountSession or credential exists.
2. **Given** the OS secure credential store is locked or unavailable, **When** the user attempts sign-in, **Then** the secure-store probe fails before the browser is opened, the app refuses sign-in with guidance naming the platform's store (Keychain / Credential Manager / Secret Service provider) and one remediation step plus **Retry**, and never falls back to storing anything in a plain file.
3. **Given** a session exists but the OS secure store cannot be read at launch, **When** the app starts, **Then** it shows the sign-in step with a warning notice "Couldn't read your saved sign-in — unlock your <store> and retry" and a **Retry** action; nothing is cleared and it is not treated as revocation.
4. **Given** the user closed the app while browser authorization was still pending, **When** they relaunch within 5 minutes of starting the attempt and the service still accepts the result, **Then** the app completes sign-in and runs the tier check; **When** the attempt is older than 5 minutes or the service rejects the result, **Then** the pending state is deleted and the sign-in step is shown with the note "Your previous sign-in didn't finish".
5. **Given** the streaming service revokes the session remotely while the user is signed in, **When** the app next detects a definitive credential-invalid response (on launch validation, refresh, or any service call), **Then** it removes the credential from the secure store, deletes all account-scoped state, and shows the sign-in step with a critical notice "Your session was revoked by the streaming service" and a **Sign in** action.
6. **Given** the credential expires and no refresh has succeeded, **When** expiry is reached, **Then** AccountSession enters `expired`, the credential is retained, the main window stays available, and a critical notification "Session expired — sign in again" with that action is raised; signing in again replaces the credential without re-showing the disclosure.
7. **Given** a sign-in attempt is pending, **When** the user starts another (e.g. presses Retry), **Then** the previous attempt's transient state is discarded and exactly one attempt remains pending.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when the user acknowledges the disclosure, then the app is force-quit before sign-in starts? → On relaunch, the disclosure acknowledgement is already recorded (no need to re-show it) and the app proceeds to the sign-in step.
- How does the system handle the user declining the disclosure on a subsequent app version after previously acknowledging an older version? → Treated as a fresh decline: the app shows the short explanation and Quit; the new version's acknowledgement is not recorded, and any existing signed-in session from before is left untouched until the user next launches and acknowledges.
- What happens if the account is downgraded from Premium to non-Premium while the app is running (not just at sign-in)? → Out of scope for this feature (EC-1.4 belongs to playback/session monitoring); this feature only defines the tier check performed immediately after authorization and the on-demand **Re-check subscription** action, which applies the same Premium / non-Premium / unknown outcomes.
- What happens when the same account signs in on another ModPlayer instance concurrently? → Out of scope for this feature; governed by the streaming service's own device rules (EC-1.5).
- How does the system handle a user opening the reachable privacy notice from the welcome screen versus from About? → Both entry points show the same in-app plain-language privacy notice content, readable offline.
- The user closes the browser tab without completing or cancelling → no signal reaches the app; the "Waiting for your browser…" state stays until the user presses **Cancel** or the 5-minute timeout fires; either path lands on the sign-in step with Retry.
- The secure store becomes unavailable *after* sign-in (e.g. keychain locked mid-session) → the in-memory session continues; a refresh that cannot persist its result is treated as a transport failure (backoff + "Sign in again" notice after 3 failures); nothing is cleared.
- Network is down when the user presses Sign in → the probe passes, the browser opens and fails to load; the app behaves as "no result": Cancel or 5-minute timeout returns to the sign-in step with Retry. The app does not attempt to detect connectivity in advance.
- The settings store is unwritable when the user acknowledges the disclosure → the acknowledgement cannot be recorded; the app raises a warning notification (per 001's settings-write failure handling) and still proceeds to sign-in for this run; the welcome screen returns on the next launch.
- Sign-out is pressed while a refresh or tier check is in flight → the in-flight operation is abandoned; its result, if it arrives, is discarded.
- The disclosure bundle version changes while the user is signed in → next launch shows the welcome screen before the signed-in flow; acknowledging keeps the session; declining quits and leaves the session untouched.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: On launch, when no local disclosure acknowledgement for the current disclosure bundle version is recorded, the system MUST show a welcome screen before any account, sign-in, Device Check, or playback UI, containing: a one-paragraph product description; a plain-language disclosure that the client connects to the streaming service through an unofficial receiver protocol; a statement that a Premium subscription is required; a statement that the user's account remains subject to the streaming service's terms; a link to the streaming service's terms (opened in the system browser); a link to the in-app privacy notice; an explicit acknowledgement action enabled immediately (no waiting period); and a decline action. Body text MAY name the streaming service descriptively. *(FR-1.1.1, NFR-11.3, GOV-6.1; Clarifications: launch order, Q-5, Q-16)*
- **FR-002**: The system MUST NOT allow the user to proceed past the welcome screen without acknowledging the disclosure. Declining MUST replace the welcome screen with a short explanation and a single **Quit** action, MUST exit the application when Quit is activated, MUST write nothing (no acknowledgement, no session, no settings change), and MUST show the welcome screen again on the next launch. *(FR-1.1.2, DP-1.1)*
- **FR-003**: On acknowledgement the system MUST record a DisclosureAcknowledgement (current disclosure bundle version identifier + acknowledged-at timestamp) in the per-user settings store using the same atomic-replace persistence as 001. The record is device-scoped: it MUST survive sign-out and revocation. If the record cannot be written, the system MUST raise a warning notification and still proceed for this run. *(FR-1.1.3, DM-27)*
- **FR-004**: The disclosure bundle version is a single explicit identifier shipped with the disclosure and privacy-notice text. The system MUST show the welcome screen on launch whenever the recorded acknowledged version is not equal to the current version. The version MUST be bumped when the substantive content of the disclosure or privacy notice changes and MUST NOT be bumped for typo-only fixes or translations; a snapshot test MUST pin the en-US source text to the version so that CI fails when the text changes without a bump. *(FR-1.1.3, NFR-11.4)*
- **FR-005**: The system MUST provide a plain-language privacy notice as an in-app text screen (readable without network access), externalized like all host strings, reachable from the welcome screen and from Settings › About; both entry points MUST show identical content. About MUST also show the product description, the app version, and a read-only view of the current disclosure (no re-acknowledgement). *(NFR-5.5)*
- **FR-006**: The system MUST NOT use the streaming service's trademarks (name, logo, or wordmark) in the product's name, icon, application identifier, or branding. Descriptive references in body text are permitted. *(NFR-11.3, C-8)*
- **FR-007**: The system MUST perform sign-in exclusively through the streaming service's own browser-based authorization flow, opened in the user's system default browser, and MUST NOT present a password field anywhere within the application itself. While waiting, the app MUST show a "Waiting for your browser…" state with **Cancel** and **Open the browser again** actions. *(FR-1.2.1, INT-1)*
- **FR-008**: After authorization completes and the credential is stored, the system MUST show a "Checking your account" state while it verifies the account's subscription tier before revealing any further sign-in-dependent UI. The check MUST time out after 30 seconds; on timeout or transport error the credential MUST be kept, the tier set to `unknown`, and the screen MUST show "Couldn't verify your subscription" with **Retry**. *(FR-1.2.3, DM-1)*
- **FR-009**: The system MUST store the resulting session credential only in the operating system's secure credential store and MUST NOT write it to a plain file or any other unencrypted local location, under any circumstance including secure-store unavailability. Non-secret session data (account identifier, display name, tier, expiry, last online validation) is stored in the per-user app state, account-scoped, holding only a reference to the credential. *(FR-1.2.2, NFR-4.1)*
- **FR-010**: When the verified account tier is Premium, the system MUST proceed to the Device Check step (001's "no confirmed device" rule) and then the main window. The Device Check first-launch rule MUST be evaluated only when the session tier is Premium. *(FR-1.2.3, J-1 step 4)*
- **FR-011**: When the verified account tier is not Premium, the system MUST show an explanation of the limitation with an **Open upgrade page** action (system browser) and a **Continue** action that opens the main window, MUST keep the user signed in, and MUST expose *playback permitted* = (tier == `premium`) for later slices to gate streaming playback on. `unknown` is gated like non-Premium but MUST be worded as "couldn't verify", never "not Premium". This slice does not gate 001's synthetic test track. Settings › Account MUST offer **Re-check subscription**, which re-runs the tier check with the same outcomes. *(FR-1.2.3, ERR-1.2)*
- **FR-012**: The system MUST allow only one account to be signed in at a time and MUST offer no add-account or switch-account action; the only path to another account is Settings › Account › **Sign out** followed by sign-in. Sign-in MUST be required to reach the main window: every signed-out state shows the sign-in step full-window. *(FR-1.2.5, A-4, J-1)*
- **FR-013**: While the app is online, the system MUST attempt to refresh the session credential no later than 5 minutes before expiry (or at half the remaining lifetime when the credential's total lifetime is under 10 minutes). On launch while online with an existing session the system MUST validate the session. *(FR-1.2.6, INT-1)*
- **FR-014**: When a refresh fails with a transport/network error, the system MUST retry with exponential backoff starting at 1 s and capped at 60 s. After 3 consecutive failures it MUST raise a non-blocking **warning** notification "Sign in again" with that action, MUST keep retrying, MUST keep using the existing credential until it actually expires (playback, where it exists, is not interrupted), and MUST clear the notification automatically when a refresh succeeds. A definitive credential-invalid response MUST take the revocation path (FR-019) instead. *(FR-1.2.6, INT-1.2)*
- **FR-015**: The system MUST offer **Sign out** in Settings › Account. It MUST first show a confirmation listing every account-scoped category that will be deleted, generated from the registry of account-scoped stores present in the build (in this slice: sign-in credential; account details). On confirmation it MUST, within 10 seconds: remove the credential from the secure store, delete AccountSession and all account-scoped state, abandon any in-flight refresh or tier check, show the sign-in step, and raise an **info** notification "Signed out. Deleted: <categories>". Plugin installations, non-account settings, and the disclosure acknowledgement MUST remain unchanged. *(FR-1.2.4, NFR-5.4)*
- **FR-016**: When the user cancels the browser authorization flow (in-app **Cancel** or a cancel reported by the service), when 5 minutes pass since the browser was opened, or when the service reports a definitive error, the system MUST return to the sign-in step with a **Retry** action and a one-line note of what happened, MUST delete the transient authorization state, and MUST leave no AccountSession or credential behind. At most one attempt is pending at a time; starting another discards the previous one. *(INT-1.1, ERR-1.3)*
- **FR-017**: Before opening the browser, the system MUST verify the operating system's secure credential store with a write-read-delete probe. If the probe fails, sign-in MUST be refused with a message that names the platform's store (macOS Keychain; Windows Credential Manager; Linux Secret Service provider such as GNOME Keyring or KWallet), gives one remediation step, and offers **Retry**; the system MUST NOT fall back to any less secure storage. If a session exists but the store cannot be read at launch, the system MUST show the sign-in step with a warning notification "Couldn't read your saved sign-in — unlock your <store> and retry" plus Retry, MUST NOT clear anything, and MUST NOT treat it as revocation. *(EC-1.6, NFR-4.1)*
- **FR-018**: The transient state needed to validate and complete an authorization result MUST be held only in the OS secure store and deleted on completion, cancel, timeout, or when a new attempt starts. On launch, if such state exists and the attempt is younger than 5 minutes, the system MUST try to complete it and, on success, proceed to the tier check; otherwise it MUST delete the state and show the sign-in step with the note "Your previous sign-in didn't finish". *(EC-1.1)*
- **FR-019**: When any launch validation, refresh, or service call returns a definitive credential-invalid/revoked response, the system MUST remove the credential from the secure store, delete AccountSession and all account-scoped state, and show the sign-in step with a **critical** notification "Your session was revoked by the streaming service" and a **Sign in** action. *(EC-1.3, INT-1.3)*
- **FR-020**: The system MUST NOT log, serialize, or display the session credential in any form outside the operating system's secure credential store; automated tests MUST run the full flow with a secure-store double and a fake authorization service and assert the credential value appears in no file, log, or notification. *(NFR-4.1, Constitution VI/VIII)*
- **FR-021**: When the credential expires without a successful refresh, AccountSession MUST enter `expired`, the credential MUST be retained, the main window MUST remain available, and a **critical** notification "Session expired — sign in again" with that action MUST be raised. Signing in again MUST replace the credential without re-showing the disclosure. *(DM-1 lifecycle, EC-1.2)*
- **FR-022**: Settings › Account MUST show, when signed in: display name, subscription tier, last online validation time, **Re-check subscription**, **Sign out**; when signed out: **Sign in**. *(FR-1.2.4, 001 Settings › Account)*
- **FR-023**: Every control introduced by this feature MUST be keyboard-operable and expose an accessible name, role, and state; all user-facing strings MUST be externalized; en-US is the only shipped locale in this slice. *(NFR-6.1, NFR-6.2, NFR-7.1; Constitution X)*

### Key Entities

- **AccountSession** *(account-scoped)*: the signed-in account's session state: account identifier, display name, subscription tier (`premium` / `free` / `unknown`), a reference to the session credential held in the OS secure store (never the credential value itself), credential expiry, last online validation time, and a read-only mirror of the acknowledged disclosure bundle version (the record of truth is DisclosureAcknowledgement). Derived fact: *playback permitted* = (tier == `premium`). Lifecycle: `signed_out → authorizing → active → expired → signed_out`; revocation and sign-out go straight to `signed_out` and delete the entity. *(DM-1)*
- **DisclosureAcknowledgement** *(device-scoped, survives sign-out)*: the locally recorded fact that the user accepted a specific disclosure bundle version: version identifier and acknowledged-at timestamp. *(DM-27)*
- **PendingAuthorization** *(transient, secure store only)*: the state of the single in-flight browser authorization attempt: attempt identifier/secret and started-at time. Exists only between opening the browser and completion/cancel/timeout; never coexists with a second attempt.
- **Account-scoped store registry**: the list of local stores tagged account-scoped, from which the sign-out confirmation and the "Deleted:" report are generated. In this slice it contains the credential and the account details; later slices register the offline cache and per-track state.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of first launches on a device with no prior local state show the welcome screen with the full disclosure before any other interactive screen is reachable.
- **SC-002**: A new user can go from launching the app for the first time to a confirmed signed-in, tier-verified state in under 2 minutes under normal network conditions, not counting time spent typing streaming-service credentials in the browser.
- **SC-003**: 100% of sign-out operations complete — with credential, offline cache, and account-scoped state fully removed — within 10 seconds of confirmation.
- **SC-004**: 0 instances of the session credential being found in any unencrypted, non-OS-secure-store location (files, logs, process memory dumps used for testing) across the test suite.
- **SC-005**: Every one of the six interrupted/revoked/expired scenarios (in-app cancel, browser cancel, 5-minute timeout, app-closed-during-auth, remote revocation, expiry) resolves to the clean, explained state defined in FR-016/018/019/021 with no orphaned session, pending-attempt, or credential data, verified by automated test against the authorization-service and secure-store doubles.
- **SC-006**: Users who change their mind and decline the disclosure never have any account, session, credential, or acknowledgement artifact left on disk afterward.
- **SC-007**: Changing the en-US disclosure or privacy-notice source text without bumping the disclosure bundle version fails CI (snapshot guard); bumping it causes 100% of previously acknowledged installs to see the welcome screen on next launch.
- **SC-008**: With the secure-store double reporting "unavailable", 100% of sign-in attempts are refused before the browser is opened, with the platform store named in the message, and zero files are written.

## Assumptions

- "Fresh install" and "first launch" are treated as equivalent to "no local disclosure acknowledgement record exists"; reinstalling the app without clearing its local data store is treated the same as a subsequent launch, not a first launch.
- The audio device check referenced as the next step after a successful Premium sign-in (FR-1.3) is delivered by 001; this feature only inserts the disclosure and sign-in steps ahead of it and restricts its first-launch rule to Premium sessions (FR-010).
- "Getting started" panel content (FR-1.4) is out of scope for this feature; it is a separate, lower-priority follow-on.
- The plain-language privacy notice's and disclosure's actual legal wording is authored/reviewed elsewhere (Q-4/Q-16); this feature ships a first plain-language draft, externalized, under disclosure bundle version 1. A later legal revision ships as a new version and re-prompts.
- Non-Premium and unknown-tier accounts remain signed in with playback not permitted indefinitely until the user re-checks, upgrades, or signs out; there is no forced sign-out for tier alone.
- "Within seconds" for sign-out (from the source prompt) is interpreted as 10 seconds or less (SC-003, FR-015), consistent with local-only deletion.
- **A-2 planning gate**: the source assumes the service's browser-based authorization yields a credential the Connect receiver (003) can use. `plan.md` MUST record the outcome of that spike in its research before tasks are generated; if it fails, the sign-in design (FR-007/FR-009) is reopened as a human decision. This spec's contract to 003 is: the credential stored under FR-009 is the one the receiver authenticates with, obtained through the same session service.
- The following are transport-level details deferred to `plan.md`, not product decisions: how the browser hands the result back to the app (loopback listener vs. custom URI scheme — the choice must satisfy FR-018), the authorization protocol variant, and the secure-store library.
